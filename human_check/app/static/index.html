Instructions

You will see one video frame from a head-mounted camera worn by a young child, and one utterance (by the child, a caregiver, or someone else) transcribed from that same moment. Speech is often not about anything on screen.

The question: does the utterance refer to a concrete, physical object that is actually visible in this frame?

  • Yes J — the object it refers to is in view. It counts even if it is small, partial, in the background, or one of many.
  • No F — small talk, the utterance is not about a concrete object, or the object is absent or unidentifiable in this frame.
  • Can't rate B — the frame or the utterance is unusable (black, blurred, unintelligible). Use sparingly.

Conventions (they match how the automatic annotation was scored, so please follow them even if they feel arbitrary):

  • Only what is in this frame. The child wearing the camera is not visible; neither is anything off screen. "Let's change your diaper" with no baby in view → No. "Leave Pepper alone" with no dog in view → No.
  • People count as objects when they are in view. "Hi, Dad" with Dad in view → Yes. Same for baby, hand, foot.
  • Names and nicknames refer to whatever they name. "Leave Pepper alone" with the dog in view → Yes.
  • Sound words count when the thing making the sound is in view. "Quack, quack" with a toy duck in view → Yes.
  • Pronouns count when you can tell what they refer to. "Press it" with a visible button being pressed → Yes. If you can't tell what "it" is → No.
  • Pictures count. "Goodnight elephant" while looking at a picture of an elephant → Yes. Read-aloud text that names nothing in the picture, or talk with no identifiable pictured referent → No. Talk about the book itself ("turn the page") → Yes, the book is the object.

Go with your first impression; a few seconds per item is fine. Z or ← goes back to fix the previous item. You can stop at any time and resume later — every answer is saved immediately. Items are served in a random order.
